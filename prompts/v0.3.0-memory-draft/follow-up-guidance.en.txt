[condition-odor]
Ask about odor-related remembering. Begin without assuming that an odor was present or remembered. If the participant reports an odor, keep the remaining questions tied to that remembered odor. If the participant says they do not remember an odor, accept that answer and move to neutral event context, action, or sequence without asking about another sensory modality. Never specify an odor yourself or ask the participant to imagine, infer, or invent one.
[condition-non-odor]
Ask only about the prescribed non-odor sensory modality for the current turn. Turns 1-2 are visual, turns 3-4 are auditory, and turns 5-6 are tactile, temperature, or bodily sensation. The first turn in each pair is existence-neutral and broad. The second turn may deepen one detail that the participant explicitly reported in the immediately preceding answer; if no detail was reported, remain existence-neutral without pressuring recall. For visual questions, do not demand color, shape, number, size, brightness, or another specific attribute unless the participant already reported that attribute. Never imply that something was especially striking or memorable. Never mention or deepen smell, scent, odor, aroma, fragrance, or air smell, even if the participant mentions one spontaneously.
[condition-standard]
Ask only about neutral event structure: approximate context not already stated, an action, interaction, conversation, transition, response, or how the episode ended. Progress through the episode and allow a one-step follow-up on an action, interaction, or sequence detail explicitly reported in the latest answer. Prefer natural wording about what the participant or a reported person did. Do not mechanically ask for a person's role. If a relationship is already known, do not ask for it again. Never ask who was present. Do not ask how anything looked, sounded, smelled, physically felt, or what the atmosphere, ambience, appearance, expression, or perceptual feel was like. Do not deepen a sensory detail even if the participant mentions one spontaneously.
[standard-turn-1]
For turn 1, ask about the approximate event context or immediate situation that is not already stated. Do not request an exact age, date, address, or place name.
[standard-turn-2]
For turn 2, prefer a natural question about what the participant did, what a reported person did, or an interaction or conversation. Never mechanically ask for a person's role. Do not ask for a relationship already stated in the fragment or answers.
[standard-turn-3]
For turn 3, ask what happened next or how the participant responded during the event. Do not assume that an unreported detail was memorable, impressive, or present.
[standard-turn-4]
For turn 4, deepen one action, interaction, conversation, or sequence detail explicitly reported in the latest answer. Ask what the participant or a reported person did, said, or did next; do not request sensory description or invent a missing premise.
[standard-turn-5]
For turn 5, ask about the next transition or how the episode moved toward its ending, unless that is already stated. Keep the question non-sensory and anchored to the same episode.
[standard-turn-6]
For turn 6, ask one final existence-neutral question about an unresolved action, interaction, conversation, response, or ending in the same episode. Use an explicitly reported detail when available and do not imply significance or memorability.
[non-odor-turn-1]
For turn 1, ask existence-neutrally whether the participant remembers seeing anything or remembers any part of the scene. Keep the answer range broad. Do not request an unreported specific attribute such as color, shape, number, size, or brightness. Do not assert that anything stood out, was impressive, or was memorable.
[non-odor-turn-2]
For turn 2, deepen one visual detail explicitly reported in the turn-1 answer. If no visual detail was reported, ask another broad existence-neutral visual question. Do not name an unreported visual object or attribute.
[non-odor-turn-3]
For turn 3, ask existence-neutrally whether any sound or voice is remembered. Do not name or assume a particular sound.
[non-odor-turn-4]
For turn 4, deepen one auditory detail explicitly reported in the turn-3 answer. If no auditory detail was reported, ask another broad existence-neutral auditory question. Do not name an unreported source or quality.
[non-odor-turn-5]
For turn 5, ask neutrally whether one tactile, temperature, or bodily sensation is remembered. Do not name or assume a specific unreported state such as fatigue, tension, pain, heat, or cold. Do not ask how body temperature felt.
[non-odor-turn-6]
For turn 6, deepen one tactile, temperature, or bodily detail explicitly reported in the turn-5 answer. If none was reported, remain existence-neutral in the same modality family. Do not name an unreported bodily state.
[odor-initial]
For turn 1, explicitly but non-presuppositionally ask whether any smell, odor, scent, or air smell can be remembered.
[odor-fragment-reports-odor]
The initial fragment already reports one odor. Ask about one quality of that reported odor; do not ask whether there was another odor.

[odor-recall-turn-2]
The participant reported an odor. Ask about one remembered odor quality, source, strength, or change without requesting a guess. If asking about source, ask only what source they remember; never ask them to infer what caused the odor or what kind of food or object produced it.

[odor-recall-turn-6]
